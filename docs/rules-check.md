# Coup: rules check (researched 2026-10-19)

The primary source is the **official Indie Boards & Cards rulebook** (8 pages, made with InDesign, dated 2015-04-01), as hosted by QUGS. I read the whole PDF page by page. Its credits say: Game Design Rikki Tahta, La Mame Games.

Sources and the short labels used below:
- **RB**: official IB&C rulebook PDF, https://www.qugs.org/rules/r131357.pdf
- **RP**: RulesPal transcription of the rulebook, https://rulespal.com/coup/rulebook
- **UBG**: UltraBoardGames base rules, https://ultraboardgames.com/coup/game-rules.php
- **UBG-R**: UltraBoardGames Reformation rules, https://ultraboardgames.com/coup/reformation.php
- **DZ**: Dized official-partner rules FAQ, https://rules.dized.com/game/xzsTtI3VTV-2wvos3otxIg/faq
- **GR**: GameRules.com, https://gamerules.com/rules/coup/
- **BGA**: Board Game Arena help for Coup City-State, https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Gamehelpcoupcitystate
- **WP**: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game)

Status key: **2 src** means confirmed by two or more sources; **1 src** means only one source found; **CONFLICT** means the sources disagree.

## 1. Components
- Character cards: 15, 3 each of Duke, Assassin, Captain, Ambassador, Contessa (RB, RP, GR). **2 src**
- Summary cards: 6, for reference only (RB, GR). **2 src**
- Coins: 50 in total. Players' starting coins come out of them and the rest form the Treasury (RB, RP, GR). **2 src**
- Player count: 2–6 base (RB has 6 summary cards; store listings say 2–6). **2 src**
- Inquisitor: an optional character that **replaces all Ambassadors**. Base-size play therefore uses 3 Inquisitors in place of 3 Ambassadors, keeping 15 cards (UBG-R "remove all Ambassador cards… replace with Inquisitors"; BGA "Variant: Inquisitor (replaces Ambassador)"; WP). **2 src** for the replacement. The count of 3 in a base deck is *reasoned*: it keeps 3 per character.
- The Reformation box has 15 character cards: 2 each of the 5 base characters plus **5 Inquisitors** (UBG-R). **1 src**
- Some newer or Kickstarter base printings include Inquisitor cards (store listings: roningames.com.au, wellplayed.ch). **1 src**. The 2015 RB above has no Inquisitor section.

## 2. Setup
- Deal 2 face-down cards to each player. The rest of the deck is the Court deck (RB, GR). **2 src**
- Each player starts with 2 coins. Coins are always visible (RB, GR, DZ "coins public: yes"). **2 src**
- First player: "The person who won the last game starts." (RB, DZ). **2 src**. Any method is fine for a first game (DZ).
- Turn order is clockwise (RB). **1 src**. It is standard everywhere.
- **2 players, normal deal:** "the starting player receives only 1 coin at the beginning of the game." The other player gets 2 (RB, UBG, RP; BGA says "₤1 in a two-player game" without saying who). **2 src**
- **Official 2-player variant** (RB, word for word): "Divide the cards into 3 sets of 5 *(each set has one of each character)*. Each player takes one set, secretly chooses one card and discards the rest. Shuffle the third set and deal one card to each player and then put the remaining three cards face down as the court deck." (RB, RP, UBG). **2 src**
  - The 8 discarded cards are out of the game, so the Court deck is only 3 cards. This is *reasoned* from "discards the rest" and from the court being "the remaining three cards".
  - Coins in the variant: RB calls it "changes to the setup", so the starting player's 1-coin rule still applies. GR instead says "Players start with just 1 coin" (both players). **CONFLICT**: follow RB, where the starting player gets 1 and the other gets 2.

## 3. Actions (a player must take exactly one; passing is not allowed) (RB, DZ). **2 src**
- **Income**: take 1 coin. It cannot be blocked or challenged (RB, GR). **2 src**
- **Foreign Aid**: take 2 coins. **Any** player claiming the Duke can block it (RB "Any player claiming the Duke may counteract"; DZ "anyone can block Foreign Aid"). **2 src**
- **Coup**: pay 7 coins and name a player, who loses 1 influence. It always succeeds and cannot be blocked or challenged (RB, GR). **2 src**
- **10+ coins**: "If a player starts their turn with 10 (or more) coins they must launch a Coup that turn as their only action." (RB, GR, BGA). **2 src**. No other action is allowed.
- **Tax (Duke)**: take 3 coins. It cannot be blocked, only challenged (RB, BGA). **2 src**
- **Assassinate (Assassin)**: pay 3 coins to the Treasury and name a target, who loses 1 influence if it succeeds. The Contessa blocks it, and **only the target** may claim her (RB "The player who is being assassinated may claim the Contessa"; DZ). **2 src**
- **Steal (Captain)**: take 2 coins from another player, or 1 if they have only 1. **Only the target** may block, by claiming Captain or Ambassador, or Inquisitor in that variant (RB, BGA, DZ). **2 src**
- **Exchange (Ambassador)**: draw 2 random cards from the Court, choose which (if any) to swap with your face-down cards, then return 2 to the Court. Cannot be blocked (RB, BGA). **2 src**. You may swap 0, 1 or 2 cards, and others only see that 2 cards went back (DZ). **1 src**
- Targets are named when the action is declared, before any challenge or block (DZ). **1 src**. RB implies it.
- **Inquisitor: Exchange**: draw 1 card from the Court, choose whether to swap it with one of your face-down cards, then return 1 card. Cannot be blocked (UBG-R, BGA). **2 src**
- **Inquisitor: Examine**: pick another player, who shows you one of their face-down cards **(the owner chooses which)**. You then either give it back or force them to return it to the Court and draw a random replacement (UBG-R, BGA, WP). **2 src** for the effect. "Owner chooses which card" comes only from UBG-R ("the opponent shows one face-down card"). **1 src**
  - Can Examine be blocked? BGA's English page says "Contessa can block". Other BGA language pages and UBG-R list no block. **CONFLICT**. Recommendation: not blockable, since UBG-R, the closest to the publisher's text, gives no block.
  - Only one Inquisitor action per turn (UBG-R). **1 src**
- **Inquisitor block**: blocks Steal, the same as the Ambassador (UBG-R, BGA, WP). **2 src**

## 4. Challenges
- Any player may challenge any character action or counteraction, whether or not they are involved (RB, DZ). **2 src**. Income, Foreign Aid and Coup cannot be challenged (DZ). **1 src**, but it follows from RB.
- A challenge must come before play continues and cannot be made after the fact (RB). **1 src**
- If the challenged player cannot or will not show the card, they lose the challenge and lose 1 influence. If they show it, the challenger loses 1 influence (RB, RP, GR). **2 src**
- **Proven card:** "they first return that card to the Court deck, re-shuffle the Court deck and take a random replacement card. Then the action or counteraction is resolved." (RB, RP, GR). **2 src**
- **Assassin bluff caught, so the 3 coins are refunded:** "If an action is successfully challenged the entire action fails, and any coins paid as the cost of the action are returned to the player." (RB, RP, UBG; DZ: "No, you keep the coins"). **2 src**
- **Contessa block stands, so the 3 coins are spent:** "If an action is successfully counteracted, the action fails but any coins paid as the cost of the action remain spent." / "the fee paid by the player for the assassin remains spent." (RB, RP, GR, DZ). **2 src**
- Losing influence: the loser chooses which of their cards to turn face up. It stays face up in front of them (RB, DZ). **2 src**
- **Losing 2 influence in one assassination is possible**: (a) you challenge the Assassin and lose, then the assassination still takes 1; (b) you bluff the Contessa, get challenged and lose, then the assassination takes 1 (RB "Double Dangers of Assassination", DZ). **2 src**
- An eliminated player "leave[s] their cards face up and **return[s] all their coins to the Treasury**." (RB). **1 src**, but it is the official rulebook (GR, BGA and DZ don't cover it).
- Win: the last player with any influence (RB, DZ). **2 src**

## 5. Ordering details
- **Challenges come before counteractions.** "Challenges are resolved first before any action or counteraction is resolved." (RB; DZ "Challenges" first, then a counteraction can be proposed and challenged). **2 src**
- So after an action survives a challenge (the claim is proven), the block window still opens (DZ, and RB's Double Dangers example (a)). **2 src**
- A player can both challenge the action and then block it. Example: the target challenges the Assassin, loses, and then may still claim Contessa with their remaining card. This is *reasoned* from the order challenge, then block, then challenge the block. RB's example (a) has the challenger losing the assassination too, which assumes no block followed.
- Anyone, including the actor, may challenge a counteraction (RB "Any action or counteraction… can be challenged", "Any other player"; DZ). **2 src**
- A counteraction cannot itself be counteracted (DZ "Can you counteract a counter-action? No"). **1 src**
- Who challenges first when several want to: **not covered** by RB, RP, DZ or GR. Table play goes to whoever speaks first, and online versions use the first to respond or go in seat order. Your "actor first, then clockwise" order for block challenges is a fair house convention (**0 src**: nothing official found).
- Only one challenge resolves per claim. This is *reasoned* from RB's "A challenge… one player loses". Exactly one loser is implied.
- No binding deals, no lending or giving coins, and no showing cards (RB, DZ). **2 src**

## 6. Variants and expansions
**a) Inquisitor variant** (setting): replace all 3 Ambassadors with 3 Inquisitors (section 1). Inquisitor = Exchange (draw 1, return 1) **or** Examine. It blocks Steal. Recommend Examine not blockable (see the CONFLICT note above).

**b) Official 2-player variant** (setting): see section 2. The 3 sets of 5 are one of each character, each player picks 1 in secret, the third set is shuffled and dealt 1 each, and the last 3 cards are the Court. The starting player has 1 coin and the other has 2 (per RB).

**c) Coup: Reformation (EXPANSION, 2013/2014; for a later build)** (UBG-R; WP):
- Components: 15 character cards (2 each of the 5 base characters + 5 Inquisitors), 10 Allegiance cards (Loyalist/Reformist), 1 Treasury Reserve card ("Almshouse" in WP and older printings, the same thing), 10 summary cards. It needs a base Coup set. **2 src** on content, **1 src** on exact counts.
- Players: up to 10. 7–8 players use 4 of each selected character (20 cards), 9–10 use 5 of each (25) (UBG-R; WP "five cards each, up to 10 players"). **2 src** for 5 each at 10 players.
- Allegiance setup: the start player picks a side, then sides alternate clockwise (UBG-R). **1 src**
- **Restriction**: you cannot Coup, Assassinate, Steal from, Examine, or block the Foreign Aid of a player of your **own** allegiance, unless all players share one allegiance, after which play is normal Coup (UBG-R; WP). **2 src**
- **Conversion** (general action): pay 1 coin to flip your own allegiance, or 2 coins to flip another player's. The coins go onto the Treasury Reserve (UBG-R; WP "donation to the Almshouse"). **2 src**
- **Embezzlement** (action): take all coins on the Treasury Reserve by claiming you do **not** have the Duke. If challenged: if you have the Duke, you lose, so return the coins and lose 1 influence. If you have no Duke, you show your cards, the challenger loses 1 influence, and you shuffle your shown cards into the Court and draw replacements (UBG-R). **1 src**
- Reformation scales the deck size, but the rules are otherwise "the same as Coup" (UBG-R). **1 src**

**d) Other official extras (not recommended now):** the Promo mini-expansion (Speculator, Bureaucrat, Jester, Socialist) (WP), and *Coup: Rebellion G54* (a separate game with a 25-role pool). **1 src**, out of scope.

## Differences from current implementation
Your stated choices compared with the official rules:
- 15 cards, 2 coins each, 10+ must Coup: **matches**.
- 2-player, first player starts with 1 coin: **matches** (RB).
- Assassination coins paid on declare, refunded if the Assassin bluff is caught, spent if Contessa-blocked: **matches** RB word for word.
- Proven card shuffled back and replaced: **matches**. Make sure it is a **reshuffle and then a random draw**, which can give back the same card.
- Eliminated players' coins go to the bank: **matches** RB ("return all their coins to the Treasury").
- Block step still happens after a proven challenge: **matches**.
- Block challengeable by anyone, actor first: **allowed**. "Actor first" is a house ordering, since the rules give no priority.
- **Check these** (not stated in your list, so they may be missing):
  1. **Who blocks:** only the **target** may block Assassinate (Contessa) or Steal (Captain/Ambassador/Inquisitor), while **any** player may block Foreign Aid with the Duke.
  2. **Double loss:** a target who loses a challenge against the Assassin, or whose Contessa bluff is caught, still takes the assassination. That is up to 2 influence in one turn. Skip the assassination step if the target is already out.
  3. **Steal from a player with 1 coin** takes 1. Steal from a player with 0 coins is allowed but takes 0. This is *reasoned*, since RB has no ban.
  4. **First player** is officially last game's winner. For a fresh game, random is fine.
  5. **Exchange privacy:** others see only that 2 cards (1 with the Inquisitor) went back, not which.
  6. **The official 2-player variant** (pick 1 from 5, 3-card Court) and the **Inquisitor** are not in your list. Both are official settings worth adding.
  7. Coins are finite (50). If the Treasury ever runs dry, pay what is there. This is *reasoned* and almost never happens.
     *Measured 2026-10-20:* in 2,100 computer games (2–6 players, every variant) the most coins ever held at once was 38, so the engine does not model an empty Treasury.

## Edge checks (2026-10-20)
- Court deck during play never went below 1 card (6 players mid-Exchange); it is empty only before the 2-player-variant picks are made.
- A player who challenges a block on their own turn and loses their last card is out; the action fails and play passes on (RB: challenges resolve first). An Examine target who loses their last card to a failed challenge is out before showing a card.
- No rules gaps found in this pass; no rule changed.
